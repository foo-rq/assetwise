How these suggestions are calculated

We compare asset mixes and cash reserves, then show up to 3 options that meet your preferences.

  • Growth aims to improve estimated growth while reducing fluctuations. If your current mix exceeds your loss limit, reducing risk takes priority.
  • Preservation and essential needs prioritise smaller potential losses.
  • We start asset allocations at 15% and 30%, scale other holdings proportionally and add cash when needed. Protected cash is retained.
  • Volatility normally needs to fall by 0.1 percentage points. A smaller reduction is accepted when repairing a breached loss limit.

Your horizon sets the comparison period. Loss checks cover historical drawdown, modelled peak-to-trough losses and the lower growth estimate. Actual losses can exceed these estimates.

Analysis limits

Estimates use historical prices. Future outcomes can differ.

  • Cash earns 0% in the model. Fees, taxes and inflation are excluded.
  • Calculations keep allocation percentages fixed. Actual holdings drift as prices change.
  • Fund holdings coverage is partial. Unknown exposure appears in the report.
Data coverage

The archive contains selected surviving assets, which can make results look better than a wider market sample. Fund holdings describe 1 dated snapshot.

Forecast assumptions

Forecasts use prices available by the displayed decision date. Your investment period sets the growth projection. Estimated returns and risks are assumed to persist; market shocks can produce larger losses.

Keeping model weights fixed assumes daily rebalancing. Trading less often changes the results.

Calculation details
  • Annual return: average daily return multiplied by 252.
  • Volatility: daily return variability multiplied by the square root of 252.
  • Maximum drawdown: the largest fall from a previous peak.
  • Sharpe: return above a Treasury-bill reference per unit of fluctuation. Monthly reference returns from the Kenneth French Data Library are converted to daily equivalents. Annualisation assumes uncorrelated daily excess returns.

Return calculations use asset prices. Fund holdings are used separately to assess exposure and overlap.
